## `fig01_fab_igg_and_cell_entry.png` — notebook 01

**Left — nirsevimab Fab vs IgG escape**, for the 3,945 substitutions measured with both formats.
Spearman ρ = 0.26. The same antibody in two formats, and the ranks barely agree: this is why the
two columns are never averaged, and why Fab is the primary benchmark endpoint. Simonich et al.
attribute the spread to bivalent IgG binding buffering the effect of mutations that reduce
monovalent Fab affinity — points falling below the 1:1 line are that buffering.

**Right — Fab escape vs F-mediated cell entry**, all measured substitutions. The red line is
`cell entry = -2.5`, the criterion published by Simonich et al. Left of it the escape estimates
scatter widely in both directions: neutralization cannot be measured meaningfully on a virus that
barely enters cells, so those values are noise rather than signal. That is what the filter removes,
and it is the largest single step in the benchmark funnel (9,296 → 4,451).

## `fig02_reference_compatibility.png` — notebook 02

**Top — the shared 574-residue frame.** RSV-A Long (DMS reference) and KX858757.1 (EVEscape
reference) align gaplessly: no insertions, no deletions, no numbering offset, so site *i* denotes
the same residue on both sides. Blue = the DMS mutagenised window (sites 26–529), green = the
nirsevimab epitope, red = the 8 positions where the wildtype residue differs.

**Bottom — substitutions per site in the benchmark.** Grey = mergeable; red = dropped because the
two references disagree at that site, 137 of 4,276 rows (3.20%). Note site **213**: it lies inside
the nirsevimab epitope, making it the one coverage gap that matters scientifically rather than
arithmetically.
